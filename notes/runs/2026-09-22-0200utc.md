## The SOL move, in full

Yesterday's run recorded a live vendor conflict on the week ending Sept 18 — **$13.2M** (SoSoValue,
via 24/7 Wall St, Sept 19) against **$60.7M** (CryptoBriefing, via Solana Compass, Sept 20) — and
resolved it by reconciling the $47.6M gap to "one disputed Thursday session that SoSoValue records as
flat," then standing on the SoSoValue series and holding the score.

**Farside has begun publishing a Solana ETF flow table** (`farside.co.uk/sol/`, fund by fund: BSOL,
VSOL, FSOL, TSOL, SOEZ, MSOL, GSOL). Its rows for that week:

| Mon 14 | Tue 15 | Wed 16 | Thu 17 | Fri 18 | Total |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|-------|
| 11.0 | 1.3 | 0.8 | 0.0 | **47.6** (BSOL) | **60.7** |

Two things follow, and the second is the one that moves the score:

1. **Farside agrees with SoSoValue on every day the two overlap** — 11.01 / 1.35 / 836,926 / no
   change. These are the same underlying series, not rival measurements.
2. **The $47.6M is on Friday Sept 18, not Thursday Sept 17.** Farside puts 0.0 on the Thursday,
   exactly as SoSoValue does. Reporting independently describes "BSOL alone attracted $48M in a
   single **Friday** session."

So there was never a vendor dispute. **The $13.2M was a truncated week** — Monday to Thursday,
published Sept 19 before the Friday row was in hand. This is trap 5.2 (rows settle incrementally,
early reads are materially incomplete) operating at the *weekly* level rather than the daily one,
and yesterday's run compounded it by inventing a disputed session to explain the gap.

The branch is a weekly print above $50M. The completed week is $60.7M on two independent routes.
**Restore fires: +0.4 -> +0.5.**

**Stated limit:** SoSoValue's own site sits behind a Cloudflare challenge that did **not** clear in
headless Chromium this run (unlike Farside, which does). Its Friday row was therefore not read
directly; the move rests on Farside's table plus reporting putting ~$48M into BSOL that day. This is
published on the deck rather than hidden.

The new week has opened at **+$26.0M on Sept 21 alone**, six of seven funds positive.

## Why the tape moved

Driver is well sourced and dated: Bitcoin closed the week to Sept 20 at **$81,159, above its 50-week
moving average (~$78,786) for the first time in 45 weeks** (Yahoo Finance, Sept 21, 07:57 CDT,
quoting Alex Thorn of Galaxy Research: "Historically, reclaiming the 50-week average predicts the end
of a bear market"). That triggered trend-follower buying into a short squeeze — **CoinGlass: ~$313M
liquidated in one hour as BTC broke $84,000, 96% of it shorts.** A second publisher gives a 24-hour
figure of **$769.80M across 115,490 traders, ~85% shorts** — a different window, both published
attributed, neither adopted as *the* number.

Macro tailwind, all Sept 21: **Brent settled $100.34** (vs $103.87 on Sept 18, -3.40%) and the
**US 10-year fell 4.1bp to 4.9560%**, on diplomatic hopes around Iran into the UN General Assembly.
