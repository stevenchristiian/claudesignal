## Corrections this run

### 1. The Hormuz zero condition is TWO observations away, not one — and this file said otherwise

§3 writes the downside leg as **zero transits on two consecutive published observations**. The series is
**6 (Sept 6), 8 (Sept 13), 1 (Sept 20)**. **None of those three is zero.** So firing requires Sept 29 **and**
Oct 6 both to read 0 — two further observations. The 09-26 and 09-27 run files, and §9's open items, all say
"**one observation from the zero condition**", which would only be true if Sept 20 had read 0.

> **The error is that a very low reading was treated as satisfying a condition written for zero.** 1 is not 0,
> and a branch that names a number means that number. This is the same discipline as §5.2's reported-0.0-vs-blank
> rule, one level up: **a near-miss on a threshold is a miss.** Corrected on the card and in §3/§9.

Note *where* this came from: it was not drafted fresh this run, it was **inherited from the notes and repeated**.
Check 9's grep caught it because the grep runs over published strings regardless of where the claim originated.
**Re-derive an inherited claim, not just a new one.**

### 2. A margin rounded the wrong way (the 09-26 checklist item, firing again)

| Old 7d | Sep 20 01:00 → Sep 27 01:00 | **-2.4669pt** | — |
| **Overlap 6d** | Sep 21 01:00 → Sep 27 01:00 | **-4.4129pt** | removing the departing day: **-1.9460pt (ROLL-OFF)** |
| New 7d | Sep 21 01:00 → Sep 28 01:00 | **-3.3884pt** | adding the new session: **+1.0246pt (NEW INFO)** |

**The two components point in opposite directions and roll-off is 1.90x the new information.** And the direction
agreement is itself an artefact: BNB was the **best of the six on the only genuinely new day** (+0.04% against a
peer median of -1.23%, **1.27pt ahead**), yet its seven-day return *fell*, because the day that dropped out was a
**+1.82%** session for BNB against a peer median of +0.74%.

> **This is the first run to measure the roll-off / new-information split numerically rather than argue it, and the
> measurement defeats option 1.** Option 1 tests one roll-off-driven number (the subject's own window return)
> against another (the margin). When the departing day dominates both, they move together and the filter passes
> while measuring nothing. **Option 2 — measure the margin change only across days present in both windows — is
> the only one of the three that addresses the cause**, and on this run it would have reported the margin change
> across the shared six days, i.e. no change at all from roll-off.
