**Three things are worth recording about how this fired.**

1. **The branch did exactly the job it was written to do.** Sixteen runs of judgement holds ended on a
   mechanical trigger, and the margin was not marginal — 8.99pt is 1.8x the bar, not a coin flip at
   the threshold. The 5pt bar was set on 2026-09-23 as "the smallest margin that is clearly not

   set conservatively rather than loosely.
2. **The relative framing was vindicated against the absolute one.** BNB is **up** +5.67% over the
   week. An absolute trigger sees a gain and fires nothing. What the relative branch caught is that
   BNB captured a little over a third of what the median of its peers captured across a window
   containing both a broad rally and a sharp reversal — i.e. it lagged on the way up *and* did not
   compensate on the way down. This is precisely the case the Sept 23 note argued the branch existed
   for, and it arrived one day later.
3. **A 0.1 cut does not cross a band.** `macroWord()` maps anything in [-0.2, +0.2] to "balanced", so
   the card's plain-English label is unchanged at -0.1. That is honest rather than awkward, and the
   card says so explicitly so a beginner is not left hunting for a visible change that is not there.

## A carried BNB figure was wrong and is corrected on the card

The deck has been publishing: *BNB Chain holds $1.0B of a $3.5B tokenized stock market, a 32.7%
share.* **Those three numbers do not divide.** $1.0B / $3.5B = **28.6%**, not 32.7%.

Checking the primary-ish source (The Coin Republic, Sept 21) resolves it: that publisher says BNB
Chain is "the only chain whose tokenized stocks capitalization is above $1 billion" and computes
**about 28.5%** of the $3.5B market. The **32.7%** figure carried in method notes §5.5 sits beside
Ethereum at $770.1M and Solana at $715.9M, and 32.7% of $3.5B implies **~$1.14B** — consistent with
"above $1B", but not with the *rounded* $1.0B the deck paired it with.

So both readings can be true; what was wrong was **asserting the division**. The deck now states the
publisher's ~28.5% and notes that 32.7% implies ~$1.14B, and says plainly that it should not have
implied the arithmetic. **This is a new failure mode for the notes:** two figures from different
sentences of different sources, each individually defensible, combined into an arithmetic claim that
neither source makes. See method notes §5.5.
