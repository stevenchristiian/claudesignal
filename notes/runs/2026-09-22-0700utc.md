**Check 7 was replaced for this run, deliberately.** The standing check is a blanked-array diff
asserting the only differences are the as-of lines. That check exists to catch *unintended* design or
JS drift, and this run's design change is intended, so it would have failed by construction and told
us nothing. It was swapped for the stronger assertion available here: extract every computation and
live-data block from both builds and require them byte-identical. That is what the blanked-array diff
was protecting, stated directly.

**Three assertions failed first and all three were the assertion's fault, not the file's** — audited
before touching `index.html`, per the checklist rule:
1. *Check 7 reported drift in `fetchChart`.* The extractor ran past the end of the function into
   whatever followed it, and a new comment below `fetchChart` read as a change. Confirmed
   byte-identical by an independent brace-matched extraction, then the extractor was fixed.
2. *Check 8 flagged `+$617.6M` and `+$147.1M` as stale.* They are cited deliberately, as the figures
   the revision replaced. A bare number is not old phrasing — the check was mis-scoped. Replaced with
   check 8b, which requires each superseded figure to appear only alongside its replacement.
3. *Check 10 wanted four `AS_OF` references.* Three is correct: one declaration, one injector for
   both HTML spans, one card label. The expectation was arbitrary.

**Check 9 caught two real claims and both were removed:**
- *"Reporting calls Sept 21 the largest daily inflow since Oct 2025."* Sources disagreed on whether
  the "since October 2025" record was daily or weekly, and the weekly figure they gave ($1.92B) does
  not match the week this deck can verify (+$6.1M). Replaced with the verified statement that Farside
  records a larger day at $1,373.8M.
- *"A fourth consecutive 24-hour window with all six up."* Overlapping windows, as above. Replaced
  with a like-for-like comparison against the 02:00 figures.
