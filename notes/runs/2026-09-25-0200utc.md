## The headline finding: a floor can be 2.4% of the row, not half of it

Method notes §5.2 carried a measured revision series of **+61.7%, +83.6%, +96.1%, +127.5%** and drew
the rule *"when IBIT or ETHA shows a blank, expect the row to roughly double."* The Sept 23 rows
settled this run and **that rule is wrong by an order of magnitude**:

| Row | Published as floor (09-24) | Settled | Revision | Floor as % of settled | Filled in |
|-----|---------------------------|---------|----------|----------------------|-----------|
| BTC Sept 23 | +$32.4M | **+$346.9M** | **+970.7%** (10.7x) | **9.3%** | IBIT +$166.3M, FBTC +$143.2M, ARKB +$5.0M |
| ETH Sept 23 | +$2.5M | **+$104.5M** | **+4,080%** (41.8x) | **2.4%** | ETHA +$50.8M, FETH +$41.3M, TETH +$4.0M, ETHV +$2.9M, EZET +$3.0M |
| SOL Sept 23 | +$5.5M | **+$13.7M** | +149.1% (2.5x) | 40.1% | VSOL +$1.5M, FSOL +$6.7M |

**Why the earlier revisions were so much smaller, and this is the refinement that matters.** The
multiplier is not a property of the series, it is a function of *which* funds are blank and how big
they are. Sept 22's BTC row had **only IBIT** missing and revised 96.1%. Sept 23's had **seven** funds
missing (IBIT, FBTC, ARKB, EZBC, BRRR, HODL, BTCW) and revised 970.7%. So:

> **Do not apply a flat multiplier to a floor. Weigh the blank funds by their `Average` column.**
> IBIT averages **$96.0M** a session and FBTC **$16.3M** on the BTC table; ETHA averages **$24.2M** and
> FETH **$4.5M** on the ETH table. A row missing IBIT *and* FBTC is missing ~$112M of expected flow on
> an average day — which on a +$32.4M floor is not a downward bias, it is the entire figure.

The honest statement, now published on the cards: **a row with IBIT or ETHA blank carries almost no
information until those funds report.** "Roughly double" understated the Sept 23 BTC row by a factor
of five and the ETH row by a factor of twenty.

## BNB: the branch held, and *why* it held is the finding

The branch cut 0.1 on 2026-09-24 at a margin of **-8.99pt** and re-armed, meaning a further move needs
a fresh 5-point divergence from that baseline. This run:

| Asset | 7d return (Sept 18 02:00 → Sept 25 02:00) |
|-------|-------------------------------------------|
| XRP   | +18.19% |
| SOL   | +14.71% |
| **BTC** | **+10.35%** ← median of the other five |
| ETH   | +9.55% |
| HYPE  | +5.52% |
| **BNB** | **+4.74%** |

Margin = **-5.61pt**. Change from the -8.99pt baseline = **+3.38pt**, short of the 5pt required.
**Does not fire; score holds at -0.1; branch stays armed.** It now sits **1.62pt** from the level that
would restore the 0.1.

**The composition of that narrowing is the part worth keeping, and it is not good news for BNB.**

| Component | Sept 24 | Sept 25 | Change |
|-----------|---------|---------|--------|
| BNB 7d return | +5.67% | **+4.74%** | **-0.93pt (worse)** |
| Median of other five | +14.65% (XRP) | **+10.35% (BTC)** | **-4.30pt** |
| Margin | -8.99pt | **-5.61pt** | +3.38pt |

BNB itself **deteriorated**. The entire 3.38pt improvement in the margin came from the peer median
falling, and that fall came from one mechanical fact: **the HYPE spike of Sept 23 rolled out of the
trailing seven-day window**, taking HYPE from +17.03% (strongest of the five) to +5.52% (second
weakest) and dragging the median from XRP's 14.65% to BTC's 10.35%.

> **New method rule.** A relative branch measured against a median can revert most of the way toward
> its trigger **while the asset it judges gets worse**, purely through what *leaves* the window. So
> "the margin narrowed" must never be reported as "the asset recovered". Decompose every margin change
> into the subject's move and the median's move before describing it, and record both.

This also partly answers carry-forward item 7 from the last run, which asked whether -8.99pt would
revert quickly. It reverted 3.38pt in one day — **38% of the way back** — but *not because BNB
improved*. So the reversion is **not** evidence that the seven-day window is too short and reading a
single bad week; it is evidence that a **median of five** is a noisy reference when one peer carries a
rolling spike. Those are different diagnoses with different fixes, and the second one is the live
concern. Do not conclude the window is wrong on this observation.

A decline of roughly **43%** in under a year, monotonic through every quarter in the series.

**Q4 2025 at $295M is above the $260M restore bar. It must not fire the branch, and the reason is the
object, not the number.** The branch reads: *"A **subsequently reported quarter** above $260M restores

is not new evidence that erosion has arrested — it is a data point from the middle of the decline, and
firing a restore on it would **invert the signal**, reading confirmation of the thesis as its
refutation. The live instrument is **Q3 2026**, which ends **Sept 30** and reports in October.

This is the same failure family as §5.5's YTD-vs-quarterly entry and the 30-day-annualised near-miss of
2026-09-24, but it is the sharpest form yet: **a figure in exactly the right series, in exactly the
right units, on the right side of the bar — and still the wrong object, because of its position in
time.** The branch's "subsequently reported" wording did the work; had it said only "a reported quarter
above $260M", this run would have wrongly restored 0.1.
